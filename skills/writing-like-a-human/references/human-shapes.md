# Human shapes

Choose a shape to fit the material you actually have. Each contract lists its parts in order and how it ends. None of them has a roadmap paragraph or a summary section. The formats marked **needs real source** appear in about 1–5% of human posts and almost never in AI output (SlopShape format audit). Use them only when the user supplies the person or the material.

## Web shapes

**1. Field note (under 800 words)**
When: one observation, opinion or lesson.
Parts:
- the specific thing that happened or that you noticed;
- what you think it means, said plainly by the speaker;
- the one practical consequence.

Ends: on the consequence, or on an open question you actually have. One heading at most.

**2. Decision guide**
When: the reader has to choose between options (tools, policies, approaches).
Parts:
- the choice, stated in one line;
- what each option is actually good for;
- **explicit decision rules** ("if more than half your late invoices sit with 3 approvers, restructure ownership; if they spread across many, adjust thresholds");
- the case where neither option fits.

Ends: on the edge case or the first thing to check. Human-leaning traits: decision rules and routing readers by their situation.

**3. Case teardown**
When: there is a real example (the user's, a customer's, a public incident).
Parts:
- the situation as it was;
- what was tried;
- what happened, with real numbers or `[NEED: …]`;
- what the speaker would do differently.

Ends: on the "differently" point. The example *is* the structure; don't bolt on generic advice.

**4. Interview or Q&A (needs real source)**
Parts:
- one-paragraph setup: who, and why them;
- question-and-answer turns in the source's own words;
- no host summary at the end.

Ends: on the last answer.

**5. Roundup of named voices (needs real source)**
Parts:
- the question put to several people;
- each person's answer, attributed;
- an optional host note where the answers disagree.

Ends: on the most divergent answer.

**6. Resource-first post**
When: the most useful thing is an artifact (template, checklist, query, script, calculator spec).
Parts:
- what it is for, in two or three lines;
- **the artifact itself**, copyable;
- notes on how to adapt it;
- where it tends to break.

Ends: on the "where it breaks" notes, or a link or placeholder to the downloadable version. Human-leaning traits: execution-support resources and a route beyond the article.

**7. What we changed and why**
When: the publisher changed a product, process or policy and has a point of view.
Parts:
- the change;
- the problem that forced it, from the team's side;
- the trade-off accepted;
- what's still unsolved.

Ends: on what's unsolved, or on how to reach the team.

**Shape anti-default:** once you've picked, ask whether this piece would look like the other nine posts on the topic. If it would, choose a different opening move or a different shape.

## General-mode contract (email, doc, memo, report, message)

1. **First sentence:** the bottom line (what changed, what you need, or what you found).
2. **Body:** the reasons and details the reader needs to act, in prose paragraphs. For an email under about 250 words, don't use bold section headings, and use at most one list, only when there are 3 or more separate things to do.
3. **Close:** the one remaining action or contact route, then the sign-off. Don't recap anything already said.

The opening is not a preview ("Here's what you need to know"), a pleasantry ("Hope you're well") or enthusiasm ("We're excited to announce").
