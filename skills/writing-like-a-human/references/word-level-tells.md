# Word-level tells (secondary pass)

**Source note:** this list is general editorial practice, **not** a SlopShape finding. The paper shows word-level fixes alone don't hide AI text. Its style-only classifier still scored 87–88 F1, though, so this layer matters once the structure is right.

Rewrite on sight:

- **The reveal:** "It isn't X. It's Y." / "This isn't about X, it's about Y." / "X? Not quite." State Y directly. This appeared 1–6 times in every baseline draft tested.
- **Reflexive triplets:** three parallel items or adjectives where one or two would carry the point.
- **Colon or dash drumroll:** "The answer: ownership." / "There's one fix — and it's simple." Just say it.
- **Stock vocabulary:** delve, landscape, seamless, robust, leverage (as a verb), game-changer, navigate (figuratively), crucial, streamline, empower, unlock, elevate, tapestry, realm, pivotal, holistic, journey. Use the plain word.
- **Hedge stacks:** "can potentially help to somewhat reduce". Keep one hedge, and only if it's true.
- **Throat-clearing openers:** "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "We're excited to announce". Start with the content.
- **Symmetric paragraphs:** every paragraph the same length, each opening with a topic sentence and closing on a punchline. Vary them; let some paragraphs be one line.
- **Evaluative padding:** "This is a powerful approach." Show what it does instead.
- **Closers:** "Ultimately,", "In short,", "At the end of the day,", "The bottom line:". These usually signal a restated-thesis ending (audit check 4).
