# Structural audit (web mode)

Answer each question about your draft literally. A "yes" means that part of the draft is AI-shaped: rebuild it using the fix. Moving a sentence doesn't count. For general mode, run only checks 4, 9 and 12, plus the general-mode contract in `human-shapes.md`.

| # | Question (yes = AI shape) | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does the title promise the reader an outcome ("How to…", "N ways…", "…and the fix")? | Retitle as a topic, a claim, a question or a concrete object. |
| 2 | Before the first body section, does the opening state the governing recommendation? | Open inside the material and let the recommendation arrive where the argument earns it. |
| 3 | Does the opening tell the reader what the piece will cover, or in what order? | Delete the roadmap. The structure should be obvious from reading. |
| 4 | Do the final editorial paragraphs summarize or restate the thesis? | End on the last new point, a concrete next action, or just stop. |
| 5 | Is there a "key takeaways", "bottom line" or "conclusion" stage? | Remove it. If something in it is new, move it to where it belongs. |
| 6 | Is the voice an impersonal explainer, with no identifiable speaker holding opinions? | Rewrite as the speaker from the shape card: first person, positions taken. |
| 7 | Does the reader leave with nothing usable beyond the prose? | Add the artifact (template, checklist, query, script) or a route to it (`[LINK: …]`). |
| 8 | Are there 4 or more body headings, or two heading levels, in under 1,500 words? | Cut to headings a reader would jump to. Merge sections. |
| 9 | Is the main guidance a numbered procedure that the reader won't actually execute in order? | Use prose or decision rules. Keep numbers only for real sequences. |
| 10 | When the reader must choose between options, is there no explicit decision rule? | Add "if X, do Y" criteria tied to observable facts. |
| 11 | Could the opening paragraph open any other post on this topic (e.g. a stock "It's the 27th. You…" scene)? | Open on something only this piece has: its claim, its object, its case. |
| 12 | Does any quote, statistic, named person, customer, study, or first-hand experience claim ("we see this all the time", "we always ask for…") come from somewhere other than the user's material or a checkable public source? | Replace it with `[NEED: what would go here]`, or restate it as an opinion. |
| 13 | Is the argument framed as replacing an old, manual or legacy way with a modern one, when the material doesn't require it? | Describe the actual trade-off instead. |
| 14 | Does it escalate from a practical annoyance to a strategic, financial or identity threat? | Keep the stakes at the size the material supports. |

Checks 1–7, 11 and 13–14 correspond to the SlopShape core features (see `structural-tells.md`). Check 8 draws on its top-ranked page-format features. Check 9 is Claude's per-model fingerprint. Check 10 is a human-specific feature. Check 12 guards against faking human evidence traits.
